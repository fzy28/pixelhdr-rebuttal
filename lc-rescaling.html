Why LC rescales noise and time

An informal picture, for intuition only

The setting

A flow model is trained to turn noise into an image. At each step, its velocity tells it which way to move. Here is the model under light 1 and under light 2:

Can we simply add the two velocities?

The image under both lights is the sum of the two single-light images. So for both lights, one might let the model follow velocity 1 + velocity 2.

What each velocity does

A velocity does two things at once: it adds light and it removes noise. Velocity 1 + velocity 2 adds the right light, but it removes the noise of both flows: 41% more noise than there is. It points past the image.

100%: all the light of the both-lights image, and all the noise the model started from

The plain sum of the two velocities is not the right velocity for the both-lights image. LC combines the two velocities with a rescaling of noise and time, which gives the right one, and trains the model under both lights to predict it. The rescaling follows from a derivation (Appendix H.1); nothing in it is tuned.

Exact in linear HDR; approximate in the Log-Gamma coordinates of the full model (Appendix H.3). Noise is drawn at reduced strength, the same in every frame, so that the frames stay readable. The frames use ideal velocities; in training, the two single-light velocities are the model's own predictions. The sphere is a synthetic scene, used for illustration only.
